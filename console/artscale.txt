# Levels whose sprite pages are drawn smaller, for levels whose art doesn't fit the console's memory at full size.
# tools/scale_pages.py makes the smaller pages (from this file when run without --atlas); tools/stage_console.py packs
# them and writes GameData/artscale.txt (console/ch360_imports.c, art_scale, reads it).
#
# "<atlas name> <percent>": shipped smaller. The pack holds that atlas's pages only at that size, under the full
# pages' names; its full pages aren't staged (the console can't draw them full size without staging again). A name
# covers every atlas it begins ("dragonlevel": dragonlevel_bg, dragonlevel_nobg, dragonlevel_rain).
# "try <atlas name> <percents>": copies at those sizes packed beside the full pages, drawn only when an artscale.txt
# next to default.xex names them ("<atlas name> <percent>", one a line); full size otherwise.
# The log's "artscale:" lines say what was used.
#
# Sizes chosen from the art each phase draws within any 2 s (tools/level_textures.py --phases, median / 90% MB; the
# levels that play smoothly draw 50-80, the console has ~195 MB of room) for the levels the user found slow on the
# console (the forty-fourth and forty-fifth runs). Soft layers (smoke, spotlights) at half size: they lose little.
#
# Glumstone: its first phase 149 / 182 at full size; at 75%, 73-83% of frames at 60 (the forty-fourth run), but its
# first phase still 20-41% in the forty-fifth: 90 / 109 at 75%, Glumstone's own art 71 of it, his attacks' pages
# coming and going (a third to a half of the 2-s sets hold each) and read again, the hold ending with a quarter of the
# first seconds' pages still out (memory). His first phase's atlas (him, his intro and attacks) at 65%: 53 MB of it.
# A line for one atlas overrides its group's (the longest name that matches).
